# About

I am a backend engineer. For the last several years most of my work has been around Java and Spring Boot: application logic, integrations, databases, messaging and the infrastructure that gradually appears around a service once it stops being a small isolated application.

Over time I became more interested not only in writing code, but in what happens around it: who will change the system later, where its boundaries are, what happens during partial failures, whether it will still be understandable six months from now and how confidently a new change can be verified.

## Work

My main stack is **Java, Spring Boot, PostgreSQL/Oracle, Redis, Kafka, Docker and OpenShift/Kubernetes**. I also use Kotlin, Python and JavaScript when a task calls for them.

I do not collect technologies for the sake of a longer list. A new tool is useful to me when it solves a concrete problem: messaging, analytics, deployment, observability or another explicit system need.

## What became important to me in engineering

I used to focus more on individual implementations: whether a method was correct, whether classes were clean and which pattern fit best. Those things still matter, but I now tend to start with broader questions.

Where is the source of truth? Who owns a piece of state? What happens during partial failure? How will the system evolve? That is why I pay a lot of attention to tests, migrations, CI/CD, observability and reproducible builds. I no longer see them as tooling around development; they are part of development itself.

At the same time, I try not to introduce complexity before it is justified. Architecture for its own sake quickly becomes another system that has to be maintained.

## AI and development

LLMs and agentic tools have become another major area of interest for me.

I use them for code analysis, prototyping, working with large contexts and automating parts of engineering workflows. I also build systems where AI is part of the product itself, such as **VillAIgence**, an experiment in server-authoritative AI villagers, Memory 2.0 and persistent NPC society inside Minecraft.

The more I work with these tools, the less I see AI as an autopilot. To me it is a powerful external capability that still needs contracts, validation, authority boundaries and deterministic behavior around it when something goes wrong.

## Personal projects

My side projects usually start with a question I want to test in practice.

Sometimes that means backend and infrastructure, sometimes AI, and sometimes game development. This is how projects such as VillAIgence, NODE ZERO and several Godot experiments appeared next to more traditional Java work.

I like taking these projects beyond the first prototype: adding tests, CI, documentation and release processes, revisiting failed assumptions and gradually turning an idea into a system that can continue evolving.

## Outside my main job

I teach software module development and testing, and I am a postgraduate researcher at Moscow Pedagogical State University. My research concerns how older preschool children can be introduced to elements of programming through age-appropriate games and activities.

Outside software engineering I am interested in philosophy, history, technology, travel and photography. Some of that also appears on this site because I want it to remain a personal space rather than only a list of technologies.
